# Map the Owner's Face Scan onto the Head — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans (superpowers:test-driven-development for the pure geometry functions). Project skill that applies: `3d-model`.

**Goal:** Turn the owner's 3D capture (`/Users/faizaan/Downloads/26_09_2026.glb`) into the site's head. The current full, closed head keeps its shape everywhere except the face, which is reshaped into the owner's, so the chrome head reads as him while still nodding, turning and lighting exactly as today. The processing must be a reusable script, because this is a "mock" export and a better scan will follow.

**What the capture is** (coordinator's inspection; verify it yourself):
- One mesh: 29,364 vertices / 49,999 triangles, with POSITION and TEXCOORD_0 only (no normals) and one base-colour texture (a photo). Units are metres: x −0.167…0.167, y 0…0.444, z −0.101…0.101. It's y-up, and the face points toward +z (the camera).
- It's a single-view 2.5D shell: the face, hair, ears, neck, a red t-shirt and the room behind (a wall above and around the head, a window at the side) are all one surface. Seen from behind, it's hollow.
- It has 4 islands: the main one with 49,393 triangles, plus 3 small fragments (388, 196 and 22 triangles) to discard.
- In the front view, the face centre is slightly left of x = 0, the chin is around y ≈ 0.25 and the top of the hair around y ≈ 0.41. The owner wears glasses; their frames are baked into the geometry around the eyes, which is fine as chrome relief.
- Coordinator renders: front/right/back/top, textured and normal-shaded. Reproduce them with a quick three.js page (GLTFLoader + `computeVertexNormals`).

**The current head:** `src/assets/head.glb`, a stock "11091_FemaleHead_V001": 51k vertices / 100k triangles with normals, meshopt + quantization (generated by glTF-Transform 4.5). `src/components/Head/index.tsx` `prepareModel()` rotates it by `(π/2, π, 0)`, scales it by 0.25 and pivots it at the neck. The material is set in code (copper chrome); the file's material is ignored.

**Approach (decided): geometry transfer onto the existing closed head**
1. **Crop the capture to the face region,** keeping the face, the front of the hair/forehead, and optionally the ears and jaw line. Remove the wall and window background, the shirt, the torso, most of the neck, and the loose islands.
   - The background sits far behind the face (flat or planar at low z) and around it in x/y. Remove it by depth relative to the face (e.g. triangles more than ~4–6 cm behind the nose-tip plane), combined with an elliptical x/y face window, a chin cut, and "largest connected component" after cutting.
   - Prefer automatic landmarks (nose tip = max z in the central window) with overridable CLI parameters. Visually verify every crop (front/side renders).
2. **Align** the cropped face to the stock head's face with a similarity transform (scale, rotation, translation):
   - Landmarks: nose tip, the two eye regions (or the eye-line height), the chin, and the face midline.
   - Then refine with a few ICP iterations restricted to the central face (cheeks, nose, forehead).
   - Work in the stock head's local space, so `prepareModel` still applies unchanged.
3. **Transfer:** for each stock-head vertex on the face side, cast a ray along the head's forward axis (both directions) against the aligned face mesh. On a hit within a tolerance, move the vertex toward the hit point by a weight `w`.
   - `w` is 1 in the face interior and falls smoothly (smoothstep) to 0 over a margin at the crop boundary, and to 0 where the hit is further than the tolerance. So there are no cliffs where the scan ends; the back and sides of the stock head are untouched.
   - Afterwards, recompute normals and optionally apply one or two passes of Laplacian smoothing only across the blend band.
   - The hair: include the front hairline and hair relief only if it looks good in chrome. The stock head is smooth and bald-ish, and a curly relief ending abruptly at the crown can look odd. Decide from renders and record a Ruling.
4. **Output** `src/assets/head.glb`: same node and axis conventions as today, so `Head/index.tsx` needs no changes or only small ones. No texture, since the chrome material is applied in code. Use meshopt + quantization like today, with the file ≤ ~600 KB and the triangle count ≤ ~120k.

**Deliverables**
- `scripts/headmap.ts`, runnable as `yarn headmap <capture.glb> [--flags]`. It regenerates `src/assets/head.glb` from the untouched stock head (keep a copy of today's head as the transfer base, e.g. `scripts/assets/base-head.glb`, so reruns never compound) and a capture.
  - Its flags cover the crop (depth cut, face window, chin/hair cuts), the blend margin and the smoothing passes.
  - It prints the landmarks it found and the fraction of face vertices moved.
  - Use `@gltf-transform/core`, `@gltf-transform/functions` and `meshoptimizer` as devDependencies (build-time only; justify in a Ruling), or plain parsing plus three.js (already installed) if that's simpler.
- **Pure geometry helpers** in `scripts/headmap/*.ts` with Vitest tests: ray–triangle intersection, the smoothstep weight falloff, the similarity transform from landmark pairs, largest-component extraction, and the depth/window crop predicate.
- **Privacy:** never commit the raw capture or its photo texture. It contains the owner's room. Read it from the path given.
